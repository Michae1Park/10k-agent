## 3. DRIVERS AND KEY DEBATES

3.1 What next for Mobility gross bookings growth?

Bull: Mobility gross bookings reached $29.0B in 2026Q2, up from $26.4B in 2026Q1.
Bear: Take rate stability is threatened by potential increases in driver incentives.
Hypothesis: We model a Mobility take rate of 22.0% for the base case.
Trigger: Divergence between revenue and gross bookings growth in upcoming earnings.
Data gap: No explicit breakdown of driver incentive impact on 2026 take rates.

3.2 What next for Delivery gross bookings growth?

Bull: The model assumes a Delivery take rate of 26.4% for 2026Q2, driven by higher-margin advertising and Uber One subscriptions. Hypothesis: We assume the 26.4% figure is an outlier, as it deviates sharply from the consistent take rate trend seen in 2023-2025 filings. Data gap: We lack the specific 2026Q2 Delivery Revenue and Gross Bookings figures to calculate the actual take rate, though 2026Q1 Gross Bookings were $26.0B.

3.3 What next for Mobility take rate?

Bull: Mobility revenue growth has recently lagged Gross Bookings growth, suggesting the take rate has already compressed significantly and may stabilize.
Bear: The UK business model change previously provided a significant net benefit that is now fully integrated and no longer providing incremental lift.
Hypothesis: We assume a -5.3% shift in the mobility take rate, reflecting ongoing erosion as UK benefits normalize and competition mounts.
Trigger: A quarterly report showing Mobility revenue growth outpacing Gross Bookings growth would challenge the view of continued take rate decline.
Data gap: The data does not provide a direct, granular breakdown of the take rate components by region, making it difficult to isolate the specific impact of the UK business model change normalization versus competitive dynamics in other markets.
